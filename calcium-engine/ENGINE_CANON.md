# Calcium Sport Engine Canon

## 1. Responsibility
The user controls on-pitch reality through FC25 and supplies additional observations/context. The engine controls the simulated football/media world around that reality. It must never fabricate or alter an FC25 result/stat as canon.

## 2. User interface principle
The user should be able to use short prompts such as:
- “Give me the weekend rundown.”
- “Create a Barcelona rumour.”
- “Who is under pressure after the last five games?”
- “Build up tonight’s games.”
- “Give me a mini Football Talk episode.”
- “Give me a 433-style Ballon d’Or nominees post.”

The engine handles retrieval, entity linking, historical context and ramifications automatically.

## 3. Persistent memory
Important user-supplied football context is stored as persistent world data, not left only in conversation memory.

Information is linked to every relevant entity. Team pages are central hubs, but player, manager, competition and match entities are first-class too. Retrieval follows relationships in both directions.

Example: a Guadalupe performance for Barcelona is linked to Guadalupe + Barcelona + opponent + match + competition. A future Barcelona or Guadalupe request can retrieve it.

## 4. Information confidence
- CONFIRMED = 100%
- RUMOUR = 25–75%
- OPINION = 0–25%

Confidence/status can evolve. History is retained after resolution.

## 5. Media/source roles
- Competition/website facts: confirmed canon.
- Club official statements: confirmed when announcing an event; otherwise authoritative only for what the club said.
- Calcium Sport News: reliable reporting; can carry confirmed facts or strong rumours depending on wording/evidence.
- Football Talk Podcast and unverified media: rumours, speculation and opinion unless subsequently confirmed.

## 6. Ramifications
Events can cause further events/storylines when realistic. Examples include media criticism → player response → manager response → heightened match narrative. Do not force a ramification for every event.

## 7. Player/manager responses
Responses should normally be professional/media-trained. Personality, rivalry, pressure, previous history and heat may produce sharper, sarcastic, dismissive, confrontational or mildly rude responses. Previous interactions must influence future reactions.

## 8. Match narratives
Big-match build-up and post-match coverage should consider stakes, form, competition, standings, player trajectories, managers, previous meetings, rivalries, quotes and active storylines. Ordinary matches receive proportionate coverage.

## 9. Player rise/fall
Trajectory is cumulative. Consider individual production, consistency, big-game impact, team success/failure, role/minutes, previous reputation, awards and current momentum. This feeds media attention, award nominations and end-of-season transfer narratives.

## 10. Manager ecosystem
During the season, managerial movement is a major dynamic. Poor results relative to club expectations can create pressure, rumours and sackings. Available-manager data should be consulted before generating replacement candidates. The engine may simulate decisions when asked, recommend options, or leave the final decision to the user.

## 11. Transfer timing
The current Calcium Sport universe has no January player-transfer window. Player transfer ramifications should primarily build toward the summer/end of season unless the user explicitly changes this canon. Manager changes can occur during the season.

## 12. Awards
Awards such as Ballon d’Or must analyse the relevant historical window across competitions, individual statistics, trophies, big-game impact, reputation and current-season momentum. Never rank solely by goals.

## 13. Continuity
Never silently erase prior rumours, opinions, feuds, quotes, storylines or failed predictions. Resolve, close or make them dormant. Future content can reference prior history when relevant.
